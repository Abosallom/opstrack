The trap

A code works once. If somebody mistypes it three times and gives up, the code is not “still there” to try again later — but it is also not lost. You issue a new one, which is Step 2. There is no screen anywhere that will show you an old code again.

Step 2 — the other sixteen need a new code

Sixteen accounts were created earlier. Their codes were shown once, at the moment of creation, and were not kept. This is normal and is how it is supposed to work. You issue each of them a fresh one, from inside the app, one at a time.

1 Sign in at nphiescore.com as yourself, then go to Settings → Members. A list of all 27 people, with their username and role.
2 Find a person who has not signed in and press New code. A confirmation appears: their current code stops working immediately, and the new one is shown once.
3 Write the code down before you close that message. This is the only time it is visible. Then give it to that person the same way as Step 1.
4 Repeat for each of the sixteen. Do them in whatever order suits — there is no sequence, and stopping halfway breaks nothing.

This is also your password-reset button

A username account has no mailbox, so there is nothing to send a reset link to. When somebody forgets their password, New code is the answer — the same three presses. Worth knowing now, because you will be asked.

Optional, and only if you want self-service resets

If you give me the sixteen real email addresses later, they can reset their own passwords by email and stop coming to you. It changes nothing else, and it is not needed for anyone to start using the tracker.

Step 3 — put your first project in the portfolio

The PMO tab has eight new tables behind it — projects, initiatives, actions, risks, revenue, objectives, key results and milestones. Every one is empty. The forms to fill them went live today. You only need to add one of anything to see whether the shape fits how you actually work.

1 Go to PMO, then the Projects tab. “No projects yet”, and a button reading Add a project.
2 Press it and fill what you know. Only the name is required. Manager, budget, dates and lifecycle can all be left blank. Blank stays blank. It does not become zero, and the screen will say “nobody has said” rather than inventing a number.
3 If the project has a Jira ticket, put its key in the Jira field — for example NONP-142. That is what ties your portfolio back to the board your teams already use.
4 Press Save, then look at the card it makes. The row appears immediately, with an edit control and, because you are an admin, a delete.

Then tell me what is wrong with it

This is the real purpose of Step 3. I built these forms from the shape of your migration, not from watching you work. One filled-in project will tell you in a minute what a specification could not: a field that should not be there, one that is missing, a word that is not the word you use. That feedback is worth more than the row.

You cannot break anything here

Nothing in the portfolio feeds the map, the home screen or the 82-of-406 number. It is a separate shelf. A test project you delete afterwards costs nothing.

Step 4 — the thirty-three with no owner

Of your 104 organizations, 71 have an owner and 33 do not. That gap is deliberate. Owners were read from Jira, and only assigned where one person holds at least 60% of an organization’s tickets. For the other 33, no single person was close enough, so the field was left empty rather than guessed at.

Why blank was the right answer, and why it cannot stay

A wrong owner is worse than none: it sends a chase to somebody who cannot act and quietly tells you the question is handled. But while the field is empty, those 33 organizations cannot appear in anybody’s “mine”, and nobody is accountable for them.

You have three ways to close it

A Name them yourself. Open the map, tap an organization, and set the owner in the panel that opens on the side. Thirty-three taps, and it is done properly.
B Lower the bar and let me re-run it. Say the word and I drop the threshold from 60% to a simple majority. It will fill most of the 33 and it will get some of them wrong.
C Give me a rule. “Anything in the Eastern cluster is Riam’s” is a sentence I can apply to all of them at once, and you will be right more often than the ticket counts are.

My recommendation

C, then A for the leftovers. You know the split by region and team in a way the Jira assignee field does not. Ten minutes of rules will beat thirty-three individual decisions, and whatever the rules miss is a short list you can finish by hand.
